Height-model membrane with roughness and cube-surface knobs

The triangular-lattice anti-ferromagnetic Ising ground states obey the one-frustrated-bond rule. Marking each frustrated bond gives a honeycomb dimer covering, and each dimer covering can be re-read as a discrete height surface: a membrane made from projected cube faces.

Effective height intuition

Instead of tracking every spin, this page animates a height field $h(x,y)$. The stiffness knob suppresses short-wavelength modes, so the surface becomes smoother. Turning on discretization makes the surface closer to a stepped cube membrane.

$$\text{AF Ising ground states}\quad \longleftrightarrow\quad \text{honeycomb dimers}\quad \longleftrightarrow\quad h(x,y)$$ $$S_{\rm eff}[h]\;\propto\;\frac{\kappa}{2}\int d^2x\,|\nabla h(x,y)|^2$$ $$\text{large }\kappa \Rightarrow \text{smoother membrane},\qquad \nabla h \text{ measures the local tilt.}$$

How to use

Drag a knob, use the mouse wheel over it, or focus it and press arrow keys. Drag the main membrane panel to rotate the camera. The discrete knob continuously morphs the Gaussian height field into an integer stepped surface.

Control–Height Model Summary

The controls are chosen to mirror the dimer-to-height interpretation: a microscopic dimer covering becomes a surface, and the long-distance theory mainly penalizes large gradients.

Control Mathematical meaning Visual effect Physical interpretation
Height scale A Multiplies the fluctuating part of h(x,y). Makes the membrane taller or flatter. Represents how strongly the coarse-grained height wanders.
Stiffness κ Suppresses high Fourier modes, like the continuum weight exp[-κ∫|∇h|²/2]. Large κ gives a smoother membrane; small κ gives a rougher membrane. Encodes the entropy-driven preference for smaller height gradients.
Tilts ∂x h, ∂y h Add a linear background slope to the height field. Leans the whole membrane in x or y. Useful proxy for different winding sectors of the dimer height.
Cube-step mix q Interpolates between continuous h and rounded integer heights. At q≈1, the membrane becomes a stepped surface. Reminds you that the microscopic dimer height is discrete before coarse graining.
Speed ω Moves phases of the Fourier modes in browser time. Animates the membrane without changing the interpretation of each frame. This is a visualization knob, not real Ising time evolution.

Rule of thumb: A changes height range, κ controls roughness, tilts choose a global slope sector, and q reminds you of the discrete cube-surface origin.
